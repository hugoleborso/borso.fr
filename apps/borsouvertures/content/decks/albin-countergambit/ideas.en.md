## 1. d4 d5

Take your share of the centre. Against 2. c4 the gambit comes next. Against the quiet systems Black builds one simple setup: knight to f6, pawn to c5, knight to c6.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5

The Albin Countergambit. Black gives the e-pawn to open lines for the pieces and to push the d-pawn to d4.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. dxe5 d4

The point of the gambit. The d4 pawn takes the c3 square from White's knight and makes e2-e3 risky. Black's plan is to win back the e5 pawn with the two knights.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. dxe5 d4 4. Nf3 Nc6

Defend d4 and attack e5. The other knight will go to e7 and then to g6, so that two knights attack the e5 pawn.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. dxe5 d4 4. Nf3 Nc6 5. g3 Nge7

The knight is on its way to g6, where it attacks e5 a second time. Black wants the pawn back before starting anything else.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. dxe5 d4 4. Nf3 Nc6 5. g3 Nge7 6. Bg2 Ng6

Two knights now attack e5 and only the f3 knight defends it. The pawn falls on the next move.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. dxe5 d4 4. Nf3 Nc6 5. g3 Nge7 6. Bg2 Ng6 7. O-O Ngxe5

Material is level again, and Black still has the strong pawn on d4.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. dxe5 d4 4. Nf3 Nc6 5. g3 Nge7 6. Bg2 Ng6 7. O-O Ngxe5 8. Nxe5 Nxe5

Recapture. The knight on e5 is central and attacks c4, and the d4 pawn still cramps White.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. dxe5 d4 4. Nf3 Nc6 5. g3 Nge7 6. Bg2 Ng6 7. O-O Ngxe5 8. Nxe5 Nxe5 9. Nd2 Be7

Develop the bishop and castle short next. If the d4 pawn needs help, ...c5 supports it.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. dxe5 d4 4. Nf3 Nc6 5. a3 Nge7

The same plan as against 5. g3. White wants b4 and Bb2 to attack d4, so Black hurries to win back e5 with ...Ng6.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. dxe5 d4 4. Nf3 Nc6 5. a3 Nge7 6. b4 Ng6

Attack e5 twice. White's queenside pawns have advanced, and they can become targets.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. dxe5 d4 4. Nf3 Nc6 5. a3 Nge7 6. b4 Ng6 7. Bb2 a5

Attack the b4 pawn before taking on e5. If it advances to b5, it pushes the c6 knight exactly where it wants to go, to e5.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. dxe5 d4 4. Nf3 Nc6 5. a3 Nge7 6. b4 Ng6 7. Bb2 a5 8. b5 Ncxe5

The knight attacked by the pawn takes on e5. Material is level, and the c5 square is now free for Black's bishop.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. dxe5 d4 4. Nf3 Nc6 5. a3 Nge7 6. b4 Ng6 7. Bb2 a5 8. b5 Ncxe5 9. Nxe5 Nxe5

Recapture with the other knight, which attacks c4. If White takes the d4 pawn, Black takes the c4 pawn and material stays level.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. dxe5 d4 4. Nf3 Nc6 5. a3 Nge7 6. b4 Ng6 7. Bb2 a5 8. b5 Ncxe5 9. Nxe5 Nxe5 10. e3 Be6

Develop with a second attack on c4. If White takes on d4, the knight takes on c4 and Black's pieces are active.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. dxe5 d4 4. Nf3 Nc6 5. Nbd2 Nge7

Again the knight leaves g8 through e7. Here it will go to f5, because White's knight is coming to b3 to attack d4.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. dxe5 d4 4. Nf3 Nc6 5. Nbd2 Nge7 6. Nb3 Nf5

Defend d4 a third time. Queen, knight and knight now hold the pawn against queen, knight and knight.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. dxe5 d4 4. Nf3 Nc6 5. Nbd2 Nge7 6. Nb3 Nf5 7. a3 Be7

Develop and prepare to castle. The e5 pawn is not running away.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. dxe5 d4 4. Nf3 Nc6 5. Nbd2 Nge7 6. Nb3 Nf5 7. a3 Be7 8. g3 a5

Threaten ...a4, which would chase the b3 knight away from the attack on d4. If White stops it with a4, the b4 square belongs to Black's pieces.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. dxe5 d4 4. e3 Bb4+

4. e3 is a mistake. Check first, so that a piece has to block on d2.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. dxe5 d4 4. e3 Bb4+ 5. Bd2 dxe3

The Lasker Trap. The bishop on b4 is attacked, but if White takes it the pawn takes on f2 with check.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. dxe5 d4 4. e3 Bb4+ 5. Bd2 dxe3 6. Bxb4 exf2+

Check. The king must go to e2, because taking the pawn would leave the queen on d1 undefended.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. dxe5 d4 4. e3 Bb4+ 5. Bd2 dxe3 6. Bxb4 exf2+ 7. Ke2 fxg1=N+

Promote to a knight, with check. A new queen would let White trade queens on d8 and then take on g1. After the knight check, 8. Rxg1 loses the queen to 8...Bg4+.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. dxe5 d4 4. e3 Bb4+ 5. Bd2 dxe3 6. Bxb4 exf2+ 7. Ke2 fxg1=N+ 8. Ke1 Qh4+

The king has stepped back, so the queen comes in with check and drives it into the open.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. dxe5 d4 4. e3 Bb4+ 5. Bd2 dxe3 6. Bxb4 exf2+ 7. Ke2 fxg1=N+ 8. Ke1 Qh4+ 9. Kd2 Nc6

Develop with an attack on the b4 bishop. White's king is stuck in the centre, and ...Bg4 and castling long come next. Black is winning.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. dxe5 d4 4. e3 Bb4+ 5. Bd2 dxe3 6. fxe3 Qh4+

White did not take the bishop, so check on h4. The f-pawn has left f2 and the king has lost its cover.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. dxe5 d4 4. e3 Bb4+ 5. Bd2 dxe3 6. fxe3 Qh4+ 7. g3 Qe4

A fork. The queen attacks the rook on h1 and the pawn on e5.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. dxe5 d4 4. e3 Bb4+ 5. Bd2 dxe3 6. fxe3 Qh4+ 7. g3 Qe4 8. Qf3 Bxd2+

Trade the bishop that was attacked on b4, with check, before taking the pawn.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. dxe5 d4 4. e3 Bb4+ 5. Bd2 dxe3 6. fxe3 Qh4+ 7. g3 Qe4 8. Qf3 Bxd2+ 9. Nxd2 Qxe5

The pawn is back. White is left with a weak pawn on e3, a target for the whole game. Develop the knights and castle.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. cxd5 Qxd5

Take back with the queen. White cannot chase it with Nc3 yet, because the d4 pawn would fall.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. cxd5 Qxd5 4. e3 exd4

Trade in the centre. White gets an isolated pawn on d4, and Black will attack it with pieces.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. cxd5 Qxd5 4. e3 exd4 5. exd4 Nc6

Attack the isolated pawn at once. White's Nc3 will gain time on the queen, so develop quickly.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. cxd5 Qxd5 4. e3 exd4 5. exd4 Nc6 6. Nf3 Bg4

Pin the knight that defends d4. Black threatens to take on f3 and then on d4.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. cxd5 Qxd5 4. e3 exd4 5. exd4 Nc6 6. Nf3 Bg4 7. Be2 Bb4+

Check. The knight that comes to c3 will be pinned, so it does not really attack the queen.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. cxd5 Qxd5 4. e3 exd4 5. exd4 Nc6 6. Nf3 Bg4 7. Be2 Bb4+ 8. Nc3 Bxf3

Remove a defender of d4. White takes back with the bishop, which attacks the queen.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. cxd5 Qxd5 4. e3 exd4 5. exd4 Nc6 6. Nf3 Bg4 7. Be2 Bb4+ 8. Nc3 Bxf3 9. Bxf3 Qc4

The key move. From c4 the queen stops White from castling and keeps the pressure on c3 and d4.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. e3 exd4

White declines. Take on d4: after 4. exd4 the pawns are symmetrical and Black has no problem.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. e3 exd4 4. exd4 Nf6

Develop. White will attack d5 with Nc3, and Black will answer by pinning that knight.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. e3 exd4 4. exd4 Nf6 5. Nc3 Bb4

Pin the knight that attacks d5. Black is ready to castle.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. e3 exd4 4. exd4 Nf6 5. Nc3 Bb4 6. Nf3 O-O

King safe first. The rook will use the open e-file.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. e3 exd4 4. exd4 Nf6 5. Nc3 Bb4 6. Nf3 O-O 7. Be2 dxc4

Take now that the bishop has moved once: it has to move again to take back. White is left with an isolated d-pawn.

## 1. d4 d5 2. c4 e5 3. e3 exd4 4. exd4 Nf6 5. Nc3 Bb4 6. Nf3 O-O 7. Be2 dxc4 8. Bxc4 Re8+

Check on the open file. White has to block, and Black plays against the isolated d4 pawn with ...Nc6 and ...Bg4.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Nf3 Nf6

No Albin here: White's knight controls e5. Develop the knight and keep the c-pawn free. 2...Nc6 would block it, and Black needs ...c5 or ...c6 against every quiet system.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Nf3 Nf6 3. c4 e6

The Queen's Gambit Declined. Support d5 with a pawn. The plan is ...Be7, castle, then free the position by trading pieces.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Nf3 Nf6 3. c4 e6 4. Nc3 Be7

Break the pin before it exists: Bg5 is coming. Then castle.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Nf3 Nf6 3. c4 e6 4. Nc3 Be7 5. Bg5 O-O

King safe. Next, ...h6 asks the bishop where it is going.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Nf3 Nf6 3. c4 e6 4. Nc3 Be7 5. Bg5 O-O 6. e3 h6

Push the bishop back to h4. The pawn on h6 also gives the king an escape square.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Nf3 Nf6 3. c4 e6 4. Nc3 Be7 5. Bg5 O-O 6. e3 h6 7. Bh4 Ne4

The Lasker Defence. The knight offers two trades at once, bishop for bishop and knight for knight. With fewer pieces on the board, Black's position is easy to play.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Nf3 Nf6 3. c4 e6 4. Nc3 Be7 5. Bg5 O-O 6. e3 h6 7. Bh4 Ne4 8. Bxe7 Qxe7

Recapture with the queen, which is now developed. The knight on e4 still offers itself to the c3 knight.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Nf3 Nf6 3. c4 e6 4. Nc3 Be7 5. Bg5 O-O 6. e3 h6 7. Bh4 Ne4 8. Bxe7 Qxe7 9. cxd5 Nxc3

Take the knight first. If Black took back on d5 at once, Nxd5 would win a pawn.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Nf3 Nf6 3. c4 e6 4. Nc3 Be7 5. Bg5 O-O 6. e3 h6 7. Bh4 Ne4 8. Bxe7 Qxe7 9. cxd5 Nxc3 10. bxc3 exd5

Now recapture. The bishop on c8 is free, two pairs of minor pieces are gone, and the game is level.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Bf4 Nf6

Against the London System, develop the knight and then attack the centre with ...c5. White's setup is slow, so Black can take space.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Bf4 Nf6 3. e3 c5

Attack d4 and open the way for the queen to b6, where it attacks the b2 pawn the bishop has left.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Bf4 Nf6 3. e3 c5 4. c3 Nc6

More pressure on d4. Knights on f6 and c6, pawns on d5 and c5: this is the setup against every quiet system.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Bf4 Nf6 3. e3 c5 4. c3 Nc6 5. Nd2 e6

Open the diagonal for the f8 bishop. It goes to d6 to challenge White's best piece, the bishop on f4.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Bf4 Nf6 3. e3 c5 4. c3 Nc6 5. Nd2 e6 6. Ngf3 Bd6

Offer the bishop trade. If White takes, the queen recaptures and Black is ready for ...e5.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Bf4 Nf6 3. e3 c5 4. c3 Nc6 5. Nd2 e6 6. Ngf3 Bd6 7. Bg3 O-O

Castle. Do not take on g3 while White has not castled: after hxg3 the rook on h1 looks straight at your king.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Bf4 Nf6 3. e3 c5 4. c3 Nc6 5. Nd2 e6 6. Ngf3 Bd6 7. Bg3 O-O 8. Bd3 b6

The c8 bishop has no future on its own diagonal. It goes to b7 and watches e4, the square White's pieces want.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Bf4 Nf6 3. e3 c5 4. c3 Nc6 5. Nd2 e6 6. Ngf3 Bd6 7. Bg3 O-O 8. Bd3 b6 9. O-O Bb7

Development is complete. Now that White has castled, taking on g3 is safe. Then ...Qe7 and ...e5 is the freeing break.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Bf4 Nf6 3. e3 c5 4. c3 Nc6 5. Nd2 e6 6. Ngf3 Bd6 7. Bxd6 Qxd6

The queen recaptures and supports the push ...e5. White's best piece is gone.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Bf4 Nf6 3. e3 c5 4. c3 Nc6 5. Nd2 e6 6. Ngf3 Bd6 7. Bxd6 Qxd6 8. Bd3 O-O

Castle before opening the centre.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Bf4 Nf6 3. e3 c5 4. c3 Nc6 5. Nd2 e6 6. Ngf3 Bd6 7. Bxd6 Qxd6 8. Bd3 O-O 9. O-O e5

The freeing break. Black takes space in the centre and the c8 bishop comes to life.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Nf3 Nf6 3. Bf4 c5

The London System with the knight already on f3. Same reply: ...c5, with ...Qb6 in mind.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Nf3 Nf6 3. Bf4 c5 4. e3 Nc6

Pressure on d4. The queen is about to come to b6.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Nf3 Nf6 3. Bf4 c5 4. e3 Nc6 5. c3 Qb6

Attack b2, which the bishop no longer defends. White's usual answer, Qb3, runs into ...c4.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Nf3 Nf6 3. Bf4 c5 4. e3 Nc6 5. c3 Qb6 6. Qb3 c4

Gain space with an attack on the queen. If White trades on b6, the a-file opens for the rook and the b-pawns advance.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Nf3 Nf6 3. Bf4 c5 4. e3 Nc6 5. c3 Qb6 6. Qb3 c4 7. Qxb6 axb6

Recapture with the a-pawn. The doubled pawns are strong here: the a8 rook has an open file, and ...b5 and ...b4 will break up White's queenside.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Nf3 Nf6 3. Bf4 c5 4. e3 Nc6 5. c3 Qb6 6. Qb3 c4 7. Qxb6 axb6 8. Nbd2 b5

Start the pawn advance. Next, ...b4 attacks c3, and the rook on a8 already looks at a2.

## 1. d4 d5 2. e3 Nf6

White keeps the c1 bishop behind its pawns. Develop, and wait to see whether he plays a Stonewall with f4 or a Colle with Nf3.

## 1. d4 d5 2. e3 Nf6 3. Bd3 c5

The same setup, ...c5 and ...Nc6. Keep the c8 bishop free: it wants to come out before ...e6.

## 1. d4 d5 2. e3 Nf6 3. Bd3 c5 4. c3 Nc6

Pressure on d4. Do not play ...e6 yet.

## 1. d4 d5 2. e3 Nf6 3. Bd3 c5 4. c3 Nc6 5. f4 Bg4

The Stonewall Attack. Bring the bishop out before closing its diagonal with ...e6. On g4 it gains time on the queen and will pin the knight on f3.

## 1. d4 d5 2. e3 Nf6 3. Bd3 c5 4. c3 Nc6 5. f4 Bg4 6. Nf3 e6

Now ...e6 is fine, because the bishop is outside the pawn chain. White's bishop on c1 is stuck behind its own pawns.

## 1. d4 d5 2. e3 Nf6 3. Bd3 c5 4. c3 Nc6 5. f4 Bg4 6. Nf3 e6 7. O-O Bd6

Develop and watch e5, the square White's knight wants.

## 1. d4 d5 2. e3 Nf6 3. Bd3 c5 4. c3 Nc6 5. f4 Bg4 6. Nf3 e6 7. O-O Bd6 8. Nbd2 O-O

Castle. Then ...Rc8 and ...cxd4 open the c-file. White's bishop on c1 stays a bad piece.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Nf3 Nf6 3. e3 c5

The Colle System. White wants Bd3, c3, Nbd2 and then the push e4. Black takes space with ...c5 first.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Nf3 Nf6 3. e3 c5 4. Bd3 Nc6

Develop and attack d4. The c8 bishop still has its diagonal, so do not play ...e6 yet.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Nf3 Nf6 3. e3 c5 4. Bd3 Nc6 5. c3 Bg4

Bring the bishop out before ...e6 and pin the knight. The pin makes the push e4 harder for White.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Nf3 Nf6 3. e3 c5 4. Bd3 Nc6 5. c3 Bg4 6. Nbd2 e6

A solid centre, with the bishop already outside. Next come ...Bd6 and castling.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Nf3 Nf6 3. e3 c5 4. Bd3 Nc6 5. c3 Bg4 6. Nbd2 e6 7. O-O Bd6

Control e5 and get ready to castle. If White pushes e4, trade pawns on d4 and e4: he is left with an isolated d-pawn.

## 1. d4 d5 2. Nf3 Nf6 3. e3 c5 4. Bd3 Nc6 5. c3 Bg4 6. Nbd2 e6 7. O-O Bd6 8. Re1 O-O

Castle. White is ready for e4. Answer it at once by trading pawns in the centre, or e5 will fork the bishop and the knight.
