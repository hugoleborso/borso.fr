## How navigation works today

- **Tabs.** Five tabs plus More. The More drawer repeats the five tabs, then Workshop (Tasks, Compositions), then Administration (Improvements, Members, Instruments), then language and account.
- **Back links.** Every back link goes to its own tab's list, not to where you came from. From "My part", back leads to Setlists.
- **Actions.** They sit in four different places:
  - a row of icons at the top (setlist editor);
  - labelled buttons under the title (song, session);
  - round buttons that hide on scroll (catalogue, setlist, setlists list);
  - a sticky bar under a form (song edit, bars).
- **Dialogs.** A second dialog replaces the first instead of opening on top of it.
- **Depth.** Tasks is 2 taps from any screen. An entry's lineup is 4 taps from the Setlists tab. A song's chords cannot be reached from a setlist at all.

## What the emulator could not prove

- **Top safe area (finding 25).** It depends on how a real iPhone applies status bar style `default` to a home screen app.
- **Tasks page width (finding 4).** Chromium widens the layout; iOS may let the page pan sideways instead. Either way the page is wider than the phone.
- **Swipe hand-off.** On iOS a swipe on the chord preview may pass to the page once the preview reaches its end.
- **Scroll lock behind dialogs.** It relies on `overflow: hidden` on `html`, which older iOS versions ignored. On those phones every dialog would scroll twice.
- **Recording, playback and the keyboard.** There is no microphone, the seeded audio files do not exist, and Chromium draws no software keyboard. What the keyboard covers on the task form, the transition note and the chart text box is not measured.
- **Window size in argent.** argent's window was about 500 px wide, not 375. I used it only for taps and took every position and size from the Playwright runs.
